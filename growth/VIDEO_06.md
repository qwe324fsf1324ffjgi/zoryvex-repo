# VIDEO 06 — "We also deleted 'ask a colleague to confirm this'"

Companion to Video 05. Post it second, two to four days later.

- **Length:** 26s · **One idea:** a reference you chose is not an independent check
- **Claimable:** yes — recorded product decision

## Hook — second zero

A contact field with a colleague's email half-typed. It is deleted, character
by character, and the field disappears.

Caption: **"This one was harder to remove."**

## Script

> "You could ask a colleague to confirm a claim. It felt fair. References are
> normal.
>
> But you pick the colleague.
>
> Nobody puts down the manager who'd say something awkward. That isn't
> dishonesty, it's just how choosing works. And a check where the subject
> chooses the checker isn't independent.
>
> So we took it out. The records that already have it keep it, because those
> people earned it honestly. Nothing new can get it.
>
> What's left is the part where somebody else holds the evidence and we go and
> ask them."

## The honest detail that makes it credible

Saying *existing peer-verified records keep their status* is what stops this
sounding like a marketing decision. It costs a sentence and it is true.

## Do not

Do not disparage references as a practice. The point is narrow and specific:
self-selected confirmation is not independent verification. References are
still useful for other things.
