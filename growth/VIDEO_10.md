# VIDEO 10 — "Candidates don't pay. Here's why that matters to you"

The business model as a trust argument. Works on both audiences at once.

- **Length:** 26s · **One idea:** who pays determines who the product serves
- **Claimable:** yes

## Hook — second zero

Plain text: **"If a job platform charges the job seeker, ask what it's selling."**

## Script

> "Candidates pay nothing here. Not to join, not to get verified, not to be
> found.
>
> Employers pay.
>
> That's not generosity, it's structure. When the person looking for work is
> the customer, the product has to keep them hopeful. Hope is what's being
> sold. Whether anyone gets hired is somebody else's problem.
>
> When the employer pays, the product has to be right. An employer who's shown
> people who can't do the work stops paying, immediately.
>
> So the incentive points at accuracy. For both of you."

## Claim discipline

- Do not name a competitor or a category of competitor
- Do not state a price in the video. Prices change; a video does not
- Do not say "free forever". Say what is true today: candidates pay nothing
- If a price is ever shown on screen, the Paystack disclosure must appear in
  the same frame, in the exact required wording

## Why it belongs last in the sequence

The first nine videos argue that the verification is real. This one explains
why the company is structured to keep it real. It only lands once someone
already believes the product does what it says.
