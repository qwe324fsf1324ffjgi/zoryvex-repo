# What actually makes this kind of content spread

Researched 2026-09-14. Read the caveat before you use any number in here.

---

## Caveat on sources, first

I searched the live web for this. Most of what comes back on "viral hooks" and
"the algorithm in 2026" is **content marketing written to rank for that
search**, published by companies selling video tools. It cites itself. The
numbers repeat across sites with no traceable origin.

So this document separates two things:

- **Grounded** — traceable to a primary source, or to a platform's own
  published guidance
- **Convergent claim** — repeated consistently across many secondary sources,
  directionally useful, **not** a measurement you should quote publicly

Zoryvex's own rules forbid publishing unevidenced claims. That rule applies to
its marketing about itself, and it should apply to the marketing playbook too.

---

## The one finding that matters most

**The first three seconds decide everything.**

TikTok for Business's own guidance is the most credible version of this: the
highest-performing videos establish their subject within the opening three
seconds, and early retention is read by ranking systems as a quality signal.
Secondary sources converge on figures around 63–71% of the decision happening
in that window. Treat the *direction* as grounded and the *decimal* as
convergent claim.

**What this means for Zoryvex specifically:** the product's entire argument is
"a CV says, a source proves." That contrast has to *be* the first frame. Not
an introduction to it. Not a logo. Not "hey guys." The contrast itself.

Practically: no script in this package opens with a greeting, a name, or a
brand. Every one opens with the claim in dispute.

---

## Retention shape, not retention average

Convergent across sources: watch time and completion drive roughly 40–50% of
ranking, with rough thresholds near 70% retention at 3 seconds, 60% at 15, 50%
at 30.

The useful part is not the thresholds. It is the **shape**. A video that holds
90% for ten seconds and then drops off a cliff performs worse than one that
declines gently throughout, even at the same average. Which means:

- Never put your best moment at the end. Put it at 60–70% and let the last
  beat be quiet.
- A 22-second video that earns a rewatch beats a 45-second one that is
  abandoned at 30.
- **Do not pad to hit a duration.** There is no length bonus.

## The uncomfortable finding

A 2026 narrative review spanning research from 2019 to 2025 found consistent
association between frequent short-form video use and attentional
fragmentation and reduced sustained focus.

I am including this because it is true and because it has a bearing on how
Zoryvex should behave, not as an aside. A company whose whole pitch is
"substance over performance" should not build a growth strategy that depends
on manufacturing compulsion.

**The line this package holds:** every video must be worth the viewer's time
even if they never click anything. Teach something true in twenty seconds. No
fake cliffhangers, no "wait for it", no rage bait, no engagement-farming
questions. If the content is only valuable as a funnel, it is not valuable.

That is a commercial decision as well as an ethical one. Zoryvex sells
verification. An audience acquired by manipulation is the wrong audience for
that product, and the contradiction is visible.

## What the market research says about the actual problem

This is the part that matters more than any hook research, because it says
whether there is anything to talk about.

**Grounded:** the 2026 Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report documents
North Korean IT-worker schemes in which operatives used stolen identities and
regionally hosted "laptop farms" to obtain real jobs at real companies. This
is a primary-source, security-industry finding, not a marketing blog.

**Convergent claims** across Nigerian recruitment sources:

- Employers increasingly verify rather than trust, and screen for competence
  rather than polished CVs
- A widely repeated hiring-manager observation: many applicants have never
  pushed code to a public repository, and employers notice
- Employers now discount certificates obtained in a weekend, and find a
  certificate compelling only when paired with a specific applied example
- Deepfake interviews and AI-voiced impersonation are an active concern in the
  Nigerian remote hiring market

**Read that list again.** It is a description of the product, written by
people who have never heard of it. The market is already saying, in its own
words, the sentence Zoryvex exists to answer: *a claim is worth nothing, a
source is worth something.*

That is the content strategy. Not a clever hook. The market has already
written the script.

### What you may and may not say publicly

| Claim | Allowed? |
| --- | --- |
| "State-sponsored fake-employee schemes are documented in the 2026 Verizon DBIR" | **Yes**, with attribution |
| "Employers are getting stricter about verification" | **Yes**, as an observation |
| "X% of Nigerian CVs are fake" | **No.** No source supports it |
| "Zoryvex customers hire N% faster" | **No.** There are no customers yet |
| "Trusted by leading companies" | **No.** Zero companies |

Zoryvex's own rules already forbid public claims of demand or traction before
evidence thresholds are met. Content is public. The rule applies here.

## Format findings worth acting on

**Convergent, low-risk to follow:**

- **Silent-first.** A large share of feed viewing starts muted. If the video
  is unintelligible without audio it loses before the hook lands. Burn in
  captions; make the first frame legible as a still.
- **Vertical, full-bleed.** A repurposed landscape video with letterboxing
  reads as repurposed.
- **One idea per video.** Two ideas produce a video about neither.
- **Native upload.** Links off-platform are down-ranked nearly everywhere.
- **Reply to comments with content.** A comment that becomes its own short is
  the cheapest good idea in this document.

**Contested, do not build on:**

- Optimal posting times. The evidence is weak and platform-specific.
- Hashtag counts. Every source says something different.
- Sound-trending strategies. Real, but volatile on a weekly cycle and not
  worth a documented process.

## What to measure

Vanity metrics are explicitly forbidden by the commercial runbook. So:

| Measure | Why |
| --- | --- |
| 3-second retention | The only number that says whether the hook worked |
| Retention *curve shape* | Tells you which second lost them |
| Saves and sends | Saves mean useful. Sends mean credible. Both beat likes |
| Profile visits per 1,000 views | Interest in the company, not the clip |
| Sign-ups attributable to a video | The only one that pays for anything |

**Not measured:** follower count, like count, "engagement rate", view count in
isolation. A video with 200,000 views and no sign-ups told you the hook was
good and the offer was invisible.

---

## The honest summary

1. Open with the contrast, in the first frame. No greeting, no logo.
2. One idea. Twenty to thirty seconds. No padding.
3. Captions always. Assume silence.
4. Teach something true even to someone who never clicks.
5. Claim nothing you cannot evidence, including about yourself.
6. Measure retention shape and sign-ups. Ignore followers.
7. The market has already written the script. Use its words.
