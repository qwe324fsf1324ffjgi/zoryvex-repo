## Single posts

1. A CV is a claim. A certification confirmed with the body that issued it is
   a source. Almost all hiring software works on the first one.

2. We removed the upload button. A file you upload is a file you control.
   Scanning it proves the file exists, not that it is true.

3. "Verify at this address" is the entire difference between a certificate and
   a picture of a certificate.

4. Microsoft and Azure certifications cannot be verified by anyone. Microsoft
   left Credly in 2024 and Microsoft Learn publishes nothing bound to the
   holder. That is not a gap in a tool.

5. You can't type your way into owning a repository.

6. Our AI cannot reject anyone. It never receives a name, a photo, or contact
   details. Switch it off and matching still works.

7. Candidates pay nothing. Employers pay. Who pays decides what a product
   optimises for.

8. A verification method a language model can pass is not a verification
   method. That is why we retired graded tasks.

9. Employers see "Candidate a1b2c3" and what we checked. They get a name after
   the candidate says yes. In that order.

10. If the candidate picks who confirms them, that's a reference. Useful. Not
    verification.

11. The 2026 Verizon DBIR documents people getting hired at real companies
    using stolen identities. Nobody broke in. They applied.

12. No OAuth. No token. No access to your private code. Publish one string in
    a public gist and repository control is settled.

13. Forked repositories don't count, and that's fair. Cloning proves you can
    clone.

14. We have no customer logos on our site because we have no customers yet. A
    verification company can't be the first unverified thing on its own page.

15. Narrow and right beats broad and decorative. Four kinds of work. That's
    all we check.

16. A certificate isn't proof you can do the job. It's proof a specific body
    tested you on specific material and will confirm it. Smaller claim. Far
    more useful.

17. "Led a team" is a sentence, not a fact.

18. The hardest product decision this year was deleting a feature people used.
    The second hardest was deleting the one that felt fair.

19. Verification doesn't make hiring faster. It changes which conversation the
    interview is.

20. Everything we can't check, we say we can't check. At the form. Not later.

21. A shortlist that says "verified in 3 of your 4 requirements, missing the
    fourth" beats one that says "no results".

22. An employer can't message you until you've said yes. That's the order the
    screens work in, not a setting.

23. Your stated minimum pay is never shown to an employer. Not summarised, not
    ranged, not inferred. Never sent.

24. Scanning an uploaded certificate proves it isn't malware. That's all it
    proves.

25. Two questions for any hiring tool: who pays, and how much of what it shows
    you did it actually check.

26. Green means checked. If it's green for any other reason, the mark is
    worthless.

27. We're not an identity-fraud product. We check specific claims against
    specific sources. Narrowing what can be faked is a real contribution and a
    limited one.

28. Turn discovery off and you vanish from every shortlist immediately. That
    switch belongs to the candidate.

29. Nobody buys a better ranking here. There's nothing to buy.

30. Most products announce what they added. Here are two we removed, and why.
    (link)

## The three threads worth writing

Only three ideas here need more than one post.

### Thread A — "How to prove you own a repository"

Six posts, each a step, screenshots on every one. The last post is the
verified state. No call to action; the mechanism is the value.

Works because it teaches something reusable outside the product.

### Thread B — "Which certifications can actually be verified, and which can't"

Five posts. One per issuer that works, then the Microsoft position stated
factually with no editorialising.

Works because it saves somebody money before an exam booking.

### Thread C — "Two features we deleted this year"

Four posts. Upload. Peer confirmation. The shared reason. What's left.

Works because subtraction is unusual and the argument is short enough to be
repeated accurately by whoever shares it.

## What not to post, ever

- Any percentage about CVs, candidates, or hiring in Nigeria
- Any Zoryvex outcome, hire, customer count, or time saved
- A named competitor
- A real person's evidence, real or invented
- A screenshot containing a real candidate's data
- A price without the exact Paystack disclosure alongside it
- A quote-tweet dunk on someone's hiring post
- Anything that reads as though it was written to be reposted rather than read
