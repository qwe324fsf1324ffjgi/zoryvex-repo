# LinkedIn posts

Twenty posts, ready to publish. Written for the person who buys, not for the
feed.

**Standing rules for all of them:**

- No emoji bullet lists, no "🚀", no single-sentence-per-line "broetry"
- No "I'll be honest" openers, no "Here's what I learned" closers
- No engagement bait ("agree?", "thoughts?", "comment YES")
- Nothing claiming a Zoryvex customer, hire, outcome, or number
- Em dashes: use a period or a colon instead
- If a price appears, the exact Paystack disclosure appears with it

Each post below is publishable as written.

---

## 1 · The founding argument

> A CV is a claim.
>
> That is not an insult. It is a description of the document. Nothing on a CV
> has been checked by anybody at the moment you read it. The dates, the
> titles, the certifications, the scope of what someone led: all of it is a
> statement by the person who benefits from you believing it.
>
> We are used to this, so we stopped noticing it.
>
> The alternative is not a better CV format. It is checking a small number of
> things against sources the candidate does not control. A certification
> confirmed with the body that issued it. Control of a public repository
> proven by publishing a one-time code where only that account could publish
> it.
>
> Two things. Both boring. Neither can be typed.
>
> That is what we built Zoryvex to do.

---

## 2 · Removing a feature

> We deleted the upload button.
>
> Candidates used to be able to attach a file as evidence: a certificate PDF,
> a screenshot, a report. It was the most-used way to add something.
>
> A file you upload is a file you control. You made it, or you could have.
> Scanning it tells us the file exists and is not malware. It says nothing
> about whether the thing inside it is true.
>
> So it is gone. The upload pipeline still exists in the codebase, unreachable
> from any screen, because deleting infrastructure is a separate decision with
> its own approval.
>
> What remains is what we can check ourselves. Fewer ways to add evidence.
> Every one of them means something.

---

## 3 · The harder deletion

> We also removed "ask a colleague to confirm this."
>
> This one was harder, because references are normal and it felt fair.
>
> But you pick the colleague. Nobody nominates the manager who would pause
> before answering. That is not dishonesty. It is what choosing is.
>
> A check where the subject selects the checker is a reference. It is not
> verification, and calling it verification is the part that mattered.
>
> Records that already hold that status keep it. Those people earned it
> honestly. Nothing new can attain it.

---

## 4 · A limitation, stated plainly

> Microsoft and Azure certifications cannot be verified. Not by us, not by
> anyone building this kind of check.
>
> Microsoft left Credly in 2024, and Microsoft Learn does not publish a
> credential bound to the holder's address. There is nothing to ask.
>
> We could have quietly accepted them and shown a green mark based on a
> screenshot. That would have been easier and dishonest.
>
> What we verify today: AWS, Google Cloud, the Linux Foundation's Kubernetes
> exams, and IBM's HashiCorp certifications.
>
> If you are choosing what to study next and you want it to be checkable, that
> list is worth knowing before you pay for the exam.

---

## 5 · The Verizon finding

> The 2026 Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report documents North Korean IT
> worker schemes: operatives using stolen identities and regionally hosted
> laptop farms to obtain real jobs at real companies.
>
> Nobody broke in. They applied, interviewed, and were hired.
>
> That is a hiring process working exactly as designed, on information that
> was never checked.
>
> You do not have to believe this is aimed at your company to notice what it
> implies. If a process can be passed by someone who is not the person on the
> CV, the process was not verifying anything. It was reading.
>
> Source: Verizon DBIR 2026.

## 6 · What an employer sees

> An employer using Zoryvex sees a shortlist that reads: Candidate a1b2c3.
>
> No name. No email. No phone number. What they see is the claim and what was
> actually checked about it.
>
> They can say they are interested. The candidate is told, and answers. Only
> after both sides have said yes does the employer get contact details.
>
> This is not a privacy policy paragraph. It is the order the screens work in,
> and it is the reason a candidate can be findable without being exposed.

---

## 7 · On AI deciding

> Our AI cannot reject anyone. That is not a setting. It is not built to.
>
> A model can summarise evidence and explain why something might fit. It never
> receives a name, a photo, or contact details, so it cannot infer anything
> from them. When it is unavailable, or unsure, or switched off, matching
> still works on a plain, explainable scorer underneath.
>
> I am not claiming it is unbiased. Nobody can claim that honestly. I am
> claiming it is explainable, and that a person makes the decision.
>
> Those are different claims, and the difference is the whole point.

---

## 8 · Who pays

> Candidates pay nothing on Zoryvex. Not to join, not to be verified, not to
> be found. Employers pay.
>
> That is structure, not generosity.
>
> When the job seeker is the customer, the product has to keep them hopeful,
> because hope is what is being sold. Whether anyone actually gets hired is
> somebody else's problem.
>
> When the employer is the customer, the product has to be right. An employer
> shown people who cannot do the work stops paying immediately.
>
> Who pays determines what the product optimises for. It is worth asking of
> any hiring platform, including this one.

---

## 9 · On narrowness

> Zoryvex checks four kinds of work: cloud engineering, DevOps and platform,
> cloud security and DevSecOps, and MLOps.
>
> That is all. Not because the others do not matter, but because verification
> only means something where we can actually reach a source, and we can reach
> a source in these.
>
> A platform that claims to verify everything verifies nothing in particular.
>
> Narrow and right beats broad and decorative.

---

## 10 · On graded tests

> We retired graded tasks as a verification method.
>
> The reasoning: a published brief marked against a published rubric can be
> solved from the brief by a language model. The mark told us nothing about
> the person, while ranking above a certification that was genuinely bound to
> their own name.
>
> A verification method that a model can pass is not a verification method. It
> is a formatting exercise.
>
> Removing it made the product weaker on paper and stronger in fact.

---

## 11 · For candidates, practical

> If you want your evidence to be checkable, two things do most of the work.
>
> One: a certification from an issuer that publishes a verification address
> bound to your name. AWS, Google Cloud, the Linux Foundation, IBM.
>
> Two: a public repository you can prove you control, where the commits are
> credited to your account and it is not a fork of somebody else's work.
>
> Neither requires paying anyone. Neither requires a platform. They are worth
> doing whether or not you ever use ours.

---

## 12 · On the close fit

> Employers ask for a candidate who matches everything. Almost nobody does.
>
> So when a shortlist is short, we show the near misses too, and say plainly
> what matches and what does not. Not to pad the list. Because "verified in
> three of your four requirements, missing the fourth" is a real, useful
> answer, and "no results" is not.
>
> The decision stays with the employer. The information should not be withheld
> to make a number look tidier.

---

## 13 · What we will not say

> Zoryvex has no customer logos on its site, no "trusted by" row, and no
> statistics about hiring outcomes.
>
> That is because we do not have them yet, and a company selling verification
> cannot be the first thing on its own site that is unverified.
>
> When there is evidence, there will be claims. In that order.

---

## 14 · On the one-time code

> Proving you control a repository takes about a minute and asks for nothing
> private.
>
> You get a one-time string. You publish it in a public gist. You give us the
> gist and the repository.
>
> We read the gist, which only your account could have published, and then we
> read the repository: public, not a fork, not empty, commits credited to you.
>
> No OAuth. No token. No access to your private code. You publish one string
> in public and it is settled.

---

## 15 · On what a certificate is for

> A certificate is not evidence that you can do the work. It is evidence that
> a specific body tested you on specific material on a specific date and is
> willing to confirm it.
>
> That is a smaller claim than most people make with one, and it is far more
> useful, because it is checkable.
>
> The problem was never that certificates are weak. It is that a PDF of one
> and a real one look identical.

---

## 16 · On saying "we cannot check this"

> When a candidate files a claim we cannot verify, we say so at the form,
> before they have spent any effort on it.
>
> It would be easy to accept it, store it, and let it sit there looking
> official. The candidate would feel better for about a week.
>
> Telling someone their evidence will not count is a worse moment and a better
> product. Everything a verification platform shows has to be something it
> actually checked, and everything it did not check has to say so.

---

## 17 · On hiring speed

> Verification does not make hiring faster. It makes the shortlist smaller and
> more expensive to produce.
>
> What it changes is what the interview is for. If the certification and the
> repository control are already settled, nobody spends the first thirty
> minutes establishing whether the CV was accurate.
>
> That is not a time-saving claim. It is a description of which conversation
> you get to have.

---

## 18 · On fraud, carefully

> Deepfaked interviews and AI-voiced impersonation are documented concerns in
> remote hiring right now.
>
> Zoryvex does not solve that. We check specific claims against specific
> sources. We are not an identity product, and saying otherwise would be
> exactly the kind of overreach we built the product to argue against.
>
> What verification does is narrow what can be faked. That is a real
> contribution and a limited one, and I would rather say so than sell the
> larger version.

---

## 19 · On building in public

> Everything I have written about how Zoryvex works can be checked against the
> product. The screens are the screens. The verification methods are the two
> that exist.
>
> That is a deliberately uncomfortable position for a founder, because it
> means I cannot describe a roadmap as if it shipped.
>
> It is also the only position available to someone selling verification.

---

## 20 · The short one

> Two questions worth asking of any hiring tool.
>
> Who pays, and what does that make it optimise for.
>
> And: of everything it shows you, how much did it actually check.

## Cadence

Two posts a week, maximum. Twenty posts is ten weeks.

**Do not** schedule all twenty. Publish four, look at what people actually
reply to, and write the next four about that. A pre-loaded queue cannot
respond to a real conversation, and the reply is usually worth more than the
post.

## What to measure

Comments from people who hire, and profile visits from them. Not impressions,
not reactions, not follower growth. A post with 40 impressions and one reply
from a head of engineering outperformed a post with 40,000 and none.
