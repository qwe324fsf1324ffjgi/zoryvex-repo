# Outreach

Cold messages to the first employers. Written to be sent by a person, one at a
time, after reading about the company.

---

## The constraint that shapes everything here

Zoryvex has **no customers, no hires, and no outcome data**. Every normal cold
email device is therefore unavailable: no social proof, no case study, no "we
helped a company like yours".

That is not a handicap. It forces the only honest structure, which happens to
also be the one that works on technical buyers: say what the product does, say
what it does not do, say what you want, and stop.

**Nothing in this folder may be sent as a bulk campaign.** Five a day,
researched individually. A verification company caught mail-merging is finished
before it starts.

---

## The founding-member position

There are five founding places, at reduced prices, and they are genuinely
limited — not a manufactured scarcity device. The founding price holds for the
life of the subscription. That is the entire offer and it should be stated in
one sentence, never built into urgency theatre.

**Do not** write "only 2 spots left!!" unless it is true and you are willing
to say what the other three went to.

## Email 1 · The direct one

**Subject:** Verified cloud engineers, not CVs

> Hi [name],
>
> You're hiring a [role title]. I'll assume you're getting a lot of
> applications and very little you can check.
>
> Zoryvex verifies two things about a candidate before you ever see them: a
> certification confirmed with the body that issued it, and control of a
> public repository proven by a one-time code only their account could
> publish. Everything else on their profile is labelled as unchecked, because
> it is.
>
> What that changes: the shortlist is short and every line on it has a source
> behind it. What it doesn't change: you still do the interview, and we never
> decide anything.
>
> Two things I should say up front. We're new, so there are no customers to
> point you at. And the marketplace is small right now, so if you need fifty
> candidates this week, this isn't it.
>
> If a shorter, checkable shortlist is worth fifteen minutes, I'll show you
> the actual screens.
>
> [name]
> zoryvex.space

**Why it works:** the two disclosures in paragraph four are the message. A
technical buyer has read a hundred cold emails that hid exactly those facts.

## Email 3 · The one that leads with the finding

Use only for security-conscious organisations.

**Subject:** DBIR 2026, and what it implies about hiring

> Hi [name],
>
> The 2026 Verizon DBIR documents North Korean IT worker schemes: stolen

> doesn't eliminate it, and I'd rather say so than oversell.
>

>
> [name]

## Email 4 · Follow-up, once

Send once, seven days later. Never a third time.

> Hi [name],
>
> Following up once on the below, then I'll leave it.
>
> If the timing's wrong or it's not relevant, that's a completely fine answer
> and you don't need to reply to give it.
>
> One thing that might be useful either way: the free role diagnostic shows
> where a brief asks for things nobody can verify. Some people have found that
> worth the ten minutes on its own.
>
> [name]

**Never send a third.** Two is persistence. Three is pressure, and pressure is
a dark pattern the runbook forbids.

Under 60 words. A long DM is not read.

## Candidate-side outreach

**Do not cold-message job seekers.** Someone looking for work is in a
vulnerable position and an unsolicited pitch reads as predatory, correctly.

The candidate side grows through the content in `growth/POSTS` and
`growth/VIDEO_*`, which people opt into. That is slower and it is the only
approach compatible with a product that says it does not exploit hopeful
people.

The one exception: replying to someone who **publicly asked** how to make
their evidence verifiable. That is answering a question, not soliciting.

---

## Rules

| Rule | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Five sends a day, maximum | Volume is the tell |
| Research each company first | A message that could go to anyone reads like it did |
| Never claim a customer or an outcome | There are none |
| Never bulk-send or mail-merge | Fatal for this product specifically |
| Two messages maximum, ever | Three is pressure |
| Price always with the exact Paystack disclosure | Required wording, no paraphrase |
| Never message a job seeker cold | They are not the customer and they are exposed |
| Answer "not interested" with "thanks, understood" | And nothing else |

---

## What to measure

Replies from people who hire. Calls booked. Nothing else.

Open rates measure subject lines and tracking pixels. Send volume measures
effort. Neither tells you whether the pitch is right, and one real reply from
a head of engineering is worth more than a hundred opens.
